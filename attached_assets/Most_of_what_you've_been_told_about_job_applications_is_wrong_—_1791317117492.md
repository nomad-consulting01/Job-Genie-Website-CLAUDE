# Most of what you've been told about why applications fail is wrong.

Not slightly wrong. The three figures you've heard most often trace back to a sales pitch, a 1974 survey of one suburb, and nothing at all.

~~75% of résumés are rejected by ATS before a human sees them.~~

No study establishes this. When 25 US recruiters were asked directly, **two** said their system auto-rejects for anything beyond knockout questions. Applicant tracking systems mostly rank. They rarely delete.

Enhancv, published November 2025 — 25 structured interviews with US recruiters, September–October 2025, across 10 named platforms. 8%, ±6 at 90% confidence. A small qualitative sample, not nationally representative. The 75% figure traces to an unsourced 2012 vendor sales pitch. [Sources](#)

~~80% of jobs are never posted — the hidden job market.~~

That number is an inflation of a 1974 study by sociologist Mark Granovetter, who found about **56%** of job-changers in one Boston suburb found work through personal contacts. Referrals and recruiter shortlists do dominate senior hiring. The 80% does not come from anywhere.

Granovetter, *Getting a Job*, 1974. One metropolitan area, one period, one method. We use the real finding and not the inflated one. [Sources](#)

~~A third of job postings are fake.~~

Postings that aren't being actively filled are real and documented. **No reliable percentage exists** for how many live listings fit that description, and anyone quoting one is quoting a survey of opinions, not a count of postings.

Congressional Research Service, April 2025, on postings maintained without immediate intent to hire. We state the phenomenon and refuse the rate. [Sources](#)

Every one of those claims makes the problem sound like something you can't control. That's why they spread.

## What the evidence does support

- **The pile roughly doubled**Applications per opening went from 116 in 2022 to 244 in 2025 across 640M+ applications at 6,000+ organisations — Greenhouse's North American customers, March 2026.
- **You're ranked, not rejected**Screening platforms sort candidates. Recruiters work down the list and stop when they have enough people to call. Nobody decides to reject you — they just never reach you.
- **The route matters more than the document**Agency-submitted candidates reach interview at a far higher rate than people who applied directly. That gap is mostly what selection looks like from the outside, not the channel being generous.
