Task 1 — add a conditional channel-evidence block to /free-autopsy3
Page: https://www.job-genie.ai/free-autopsy3
What the page already does: four short questions (goal, situation, timeline, channels), then a reflection screen the page describes as "see your answers together" — explicitly framed as "a reflection on your search channels, not a prediction about an employer's decision."
What to add: one short evidence block on the reflection screen, shown only when the user's channel answer indicates public job boards.
Why here and nowhere else. Channel is this page's actual subject. The block must appear after the four questions, never on the intro screen — someone who has just told us where their applications went is primed for it in a way a cold visitor is not. Do not add this copy to /free-autopsy, /free-autopsy2 or /free-autopsy4.
The conditional logic
The block is conditional on the channel answer. This is not optional polish — it is the point.
Show the block when the user's channel answer includes public job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, "job boards", "mostly job boards", or whatever the existing option labels are).
Suppress the block entirely when the answer is specialist recruiters, referrals, direct to company careers pages, personal network, or any option that is not a public board. No fallback copy, no alternative quote — the section simply does not render.
Why suppression matters. If someone answers "specialist recruiters" and we show them an argument about job boards, we reveal the reflection as a script rather than a response. The page's entire credibility rests on the reflection appearing to react to what they said. One mismatched block undoes that.
If the channel question allows multiple selections, show the block only when public boards are the dominant or sole answer. If boards are one of several and not dominant, suppress.
The copy — insert verbatim
Use this exactly. Do not reword, shorten, expand, or add a heading beyond the one given. Do not add a call to action inside the block.
You said most of these went to public job boards.
That's worth knowing because of who's on the other side. A social recruiting platform tells employers plainly that job boards reach "only active candidates" and place their role "alongside competing roles" — and sells them a pipeline built before the posting goes live.
Three other employer-side vendors say versions of the same thing. None of them is talking to candidates.
Link requirement. The phrase a social recruiting platform links to the methodology page's channel section, not to the vendor's own blog. The vendor is not named in body copy — the quoted fragments stay in quotation marks and the attribution is by category.
The last line carries the block. "None of them is talking to candidates" is the reframe: this is not advice about channels, it is what the other side says when they think you are not listening. If anything gets cut for length, cut from the middle paragraph and keep that sentence.
Placement, styling, and what not to touch
Placement. On the reflection screen, after the user's own answers are displayed back and before the optional email signup. It is corroboration for something the page has already said — it must not appear above the reflection itself.
Styling. Visually quieter than the reflection, not louder. No card, no coloured panel, no icon, no badge. A left rule or a simple indent is enough. It is a footnote to their answer, not a feature.

Task 2 — resolve the indexing conflict across the autopsy pages
Independent of Task 1. Ship it separately if that is easier.
The problem. /free-autopsy, /free-autopsy2, /free-autopsy3 and /free-autopsy4 are all index, follow, each canonical to itself, and all four share the same og:image and the same og:image:alt. If they are A/B variants they are competing for the same queries and splitting signal, and a share of any of them produces an identical card.
This needs a decision before any code changes. Do not guess — report which case applies and wait.
Case A — they are A/B variants of one page. Choose the canonical (likely /free-autopsy), set <link rel="canonical"> on all four to that URL, and set <meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow"> on the other three. Leave the canonical one indexed.
Case B — they are distinct pages for distinct ad sets. Keep all four indexed and self-canonical, but give each its own og:image, og:image:alt, og:title and og:description matching that page's actual argument — the filter, ranking, channel, product preview. The current shared card is wrong for three of the four.
Either way: /free-autopsy4 is a product preview with sample data. If it stays indexed it needs its own card and description making the preview nature obvious before the click.
