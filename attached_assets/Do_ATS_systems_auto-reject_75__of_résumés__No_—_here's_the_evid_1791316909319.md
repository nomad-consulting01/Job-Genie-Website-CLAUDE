# Do applicant tracking systems auto-reject 75% of résumés?

Short answer

No. No study establishes that figure. In the only recent attempt to ask recruiters directly, 2 of 25 said their system auto-rejects for anything beyond knockout questions. Applicant tracking systems mostly rank candidates. They rarely delete them.

The 75% number traces to an unsourced vendor sales pitch from 2012. It has been repeated since without anyone re-establishing it.

Last reviewed 6 October 2026 · Sample sizes and limitations stated throughout

## Where the 75% figure comes from

It does not come from a study. It appears in marketing material produced by résumé-optimisation vendors — companies whose product exists to solve the problem the figure describes — and the earliest traceable appearance is a 2012 sales deck with no citation attached.

Every later repetition cites a repetition. Follow any chain of links far enough and it terminates at a blog post, not a dataset. That is the whole provenance.

| Claim | Primary source | Holds up? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 75% of résumés rejected by ATS | Unsourced 2012 vendor sales material | No — no study exists |
| Recruiters whose system auto-rejects | Enhancv, Nov 2025 — 25 structured interviews | 2 of 25, roughly 8%, ±6 at 90% confidence |
| Applications per opening | Greenhouse, March 2026 — 640M+ applications, 6,000+ organisations | 116 in 2022 to 244 in 2025 |

The Enhancv work is 25 recruiters interviewed across 10 named platforms in September and October 2025. It is a small qualitative sample and not nationally representative. We cite it because it is the only recent attempt to ask the question directly — not because 25 interviews settle it.

## What these systems actually do

### Can an ATS reject an application automatically?

Yes, in one narrow case. Knockout questions — work authorisation, a required licence, a minimum years-of-experience answer — can filter an application before a human sees it. Those are questions you answered, not inferences the system drew from your document.

Beyond knockout questions, 2 of the 25 recruiters interviewed described any automatic rejection. [Enhancv, November 2025](#)

### So what happens to the other applications?

They are scored and ordered. A recruiter opens a ranked list, works down it, and stops when they have enough people to call. Nothing rejected you. The list simply ran out of attention before it reached you.

This matters because the two situations call for different responses. If a machine deleted your file, formatting is the fix. If a human never got far enough down the list, position is the fix — and position is mostly about evidence and route, not fonts.

### Does keyword matching exist at all?

Yes. Keyword and skill matching is a real scoring input, and a résumé that never states a requirement the posting names will rank lower for it. That is a ranking penalty, not a deletion. It is worth fixing. It is not worth being frightened of.

### Do résumé formats break parsing?

Occasionally, and the cases are specific rather than general: text inside images, multi-column layouts that interleave when read linearly, tables used for layout, critical detail in headers or footers. A single-column document in a standard file format clears nearly all of it.

What this does not justify is paying for an "ATS-optimised" rebuild of a document that already parses. If you want to know whether yours parses, paste it into a plain text editor and read what comes out.

### Then why do so few applications get replies?

The pile roughly doubled. Applications per opening went from 116 in 2022 to 244 in 2025 across more than 640 million applications at over 6,000 organisations. Volume at that level produces silence without anyone deciding anything about you.

Greenhouse, March 2026. North American customers of one platform — a large sample, not a census of the market. [Source](#)

The 75% claim survives because it is comforting. It makes the problem a machine's fault and the fix a formatting job.

That is the commercial reason it gets repeated. A candidate who believes a robot deleted their CV will buy a robot-proof CV. A candidate who understands they were ranked 180th out of 244 has a harder, more useful problem to work on.

## What the evidence supports doing instead

1. **Check parsing once, then stop**Single column, standard file format, no text in images, nothing critical in a header. Confirm it reads correctly as plain text and never think about it again.
2. **State evidence rather than implying it**Ranking rewards what is stated. Most CVs imply scope, budget, headcount and outcomes and never put a number on the page.
3. **Answer the knockout questions accurately**This is the one place automatic filtering genuinely operates. A blank or careless answer here can do what the 75% claim is wrongly blamed for.
4. **Change the route, not just the document**Agency-submitted candidates reach interview at far higher rates than direct applicants. Part of that gap is the filter rather than the channel — but the pile you are standing in is smaller either way.
